## Statement

**Shelfie Books** introduced **free shipping on orders over 40 €**. A month later, **average order
value (AOV) is up 15 %**. The team wants to call the change a success.

What is the best response?

## Options

- Agree: a 15 % higher AOV means more revenue per customer.
- Agree, but only if the increase is statistically significant.
- Disagree: AOV should never be used as a metric.
- Check orders and revenue per visitor: AOV can rise while total revenue falls if small orders disappear.

## Hints

### Hint 1

AOV is a ratio: revenue divided by orders. What happens to it if the cheapest orders stop happening?

## Explanation

**AOV = revenue / orders.** It goes up when orders get bigger, but also when **small orders
disappear**. A free-shipping threshold does both: some customers add a book to reach 40 €, and others
who wanted a single 12 € book now find shipping expensive by comparison and leave.

The decision metric is **revenue per visitor** (conversion × AOV), plus total orders and margin (free
shipping has a cost). Then AOV explains *how* revenue changed.

Ratio metrics are easy to game and easy to misread: always look at the numerator and the denominator
separately. Significance (B) doesn't solve a wrong metric, and AOV is useful as a diagnostic (C goes
too far).
